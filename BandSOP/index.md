## Chapter 1. Purpose, Scope, and Operating Principles

### 1-1. Purpose
Establish repeatable procedures for the safe, organized movement and support of a high school marching band of approximately 250 students at competitions and other off-campus events. Provide clear duties, equipment accountability, meal and water support, and checklists suitable for use in the field.

### 1-2. Scope
This SOP covers preparation, loading, transportation, unloading, staging, performance support, meals, minor repairs, recovery, and return. The transportation package consists of one semi trailer for band equipment and two smaller pickup-towed trailers for props and electronics. Actual contents and vehicle assignments are recorded for each event.

### 1-3. Operating principles
1. Protect students, volunteers, staff, and the public.
2. Assign one accountable owner to each task and name a backup for critical duties.
3. Use a written inventory and load plan; verify completion rather than assume it.
4. Keep vehicle operations and heavy equipment movement under trained adult control.
5. Plan support around the event schedule and actual attendance.
6. Report deficiencies promptly and record corrective action.

### 1-4. Relationship to school procedures
School and district requirements, venue instructions, vehicle operating requirements, and emergency procedures govern the activity. This draft does not authorize volunteers to assume student supervision, medical, driving, or repair duties beyond their assigned qualifications and school authorization. Resolve conflicting instructions through the Head Band Director before execution.

## Chapter 2. Command, Organization, and Responsibilities

### 2-1. Director hierarchy
The band has **three band directors**:

1. **Head Band Director:** Overall authority for the band program and event mission. Approves priorities, schedules, significant changes, and final event execution decisions.
2. **Designated Assistant Band Director — Equipment:** Reports to the Head Band Director and has direct responsibility for overall equipment and load management, equipment safety, and functionality. Ensures that all equipment is properly loaded and unloaded and verifies that required checks are complete before release for movement or use.
3. **Other Assistant Band Director:** Performs instructional, student-management, or event duties assigned by the Head Band Director. Supports coordination with the road crew as directed. This role does not automatically share or replace the designated equipment assistant’s accountability.

Record director names and the designated equipment assistant in Appendix B. Any temporary replacement must be explicitly designated by the Head Band Director and briefed on outstanding deficiencies.

### 2-2. Road Crew Chief
Reports through the designated equipment assistant. Organizes volunteers, assigns section leads, runs crew briefings, tracks checklists, and coordinates timing. Reports load status, shortages, damage, and safety concerns. Crew task execution supports, but does not transfer, the designated assistant director’s equipment responsibility.

### 2-3. Functional leads
| Position | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Load/Unload Lead | Executes approved load sequence, controls ramps and trailer access, reconciles inventory |
| Drivers | Inspect assigned vehicle and trailer, verify transport readiness, control vehicle movement |
| Prop Lead | Accounts for props, checks assembly and stability, coordinates movement and storage |
| Electronics Lead | Accounts for electronics, checks connections and protection, coordinates functional checks |
| Performance Support Lead | Coordinates staging, equipment placement, field entry, and recovery with directors |
| Meal Support Lead | Confirms attendance, vendor order, event-specific dietary assessment, and distribution |
| Sustainment Lead | Provides drinking water, coolers, tables, serving supplies, sanitation, and cleanup |
| Repair Lead | Controls toolbox, performs authorized minor repairs, records defects and repair status |
| Safety Lead | Observes hazards, briefs controls, coordinates emergency information and incident reporting |

Positions may be combined when workload permits. Every task still requires a named owner. Avoid simultaneous assignments that leave vehicle movement, students, serving operations, or equipment handling without coverage.

### 2-4. All crew members
Attend briefings; follow assigned leads; use appropriate handling methods and protective equipment; report injury, damage, missing equipment, and hazards; and stop an unsafe action. Resume a stopped task only after the hazard is addressed and the responsible lead approves.

## Chapter 3. Event Planning and Coordination

### 3-1. Establish the mission
Complete Appendix A for each event. Confirm event location, attendance, departure and return windows, check-in procedures, parking, unloading access, warm-up, performance, meal periods, awards, and weather alternatives.

### 3-2. Planning sequence
1. Obtain the director-approved event schedule and required equipment list.
2. Confirm drivers, towing vehicles, trailers, crew availability, and backups.
3. Review venue access, surface conditions, ramp space, equipment routes, and restrictions.
4. Establish the load plan and identify unusual props or additional equipment.
5. Confirm meal counts, menu, dietary needs for each meal, water, tables, and serving supplies.
6. Assign responsibilities and communicate report times.
7. Conduct the final readiness review and brief changes before departure.

### 3-3. Changes
The Road Crew Chief records schedule, personnel, vendor, or load changes and informs affected leads. The designated equipment assistant approves equipment and load changes. Escalate changes affecting overall timing, safety, or program priorities to the Head Band Director.

## Chapter 4. Personnel, Assignments, and Communications

### 4-1. Accountability
Maintain a roster of crew members, drivers, leads, backups, report times, and contact information in Appendix B. Confirm attendance at initial report, before departure, before venue departure, and at release. Student attendance remains with assigned school staff or authorized chaperones.

### 4-2. Briefing
The Road Crew Chief briefs mission, schedule, assignments, load sequence, movement routes, meal arrangements, weather, emergency procedures, and communication method. Each lead confirms understanding and reports shortages or limitations.

### 4-3. Communications
Use the school-approved group communication method and assigned radios where available. Record the primary channel, alternate method, and key contacts. Keep messages short: identify sender, location, issue, and requested action. Protect student medical and dietary information; distribute only what authorized personnel need to perform their duties.

### 4-4. Status reporting
Use clear status terms: **Ready**, **Ready with limitation** (state limitation and approving authority), or **Not ready**. Report equipment readiness to the designated equipment assistant. Silence is not confirmation of completion.

## Chapter 5. Equipment Accountability and Load Planning

### 5-1. Master inventory
Maintain a current inventory of instruments and cases handled by the crew, carts, props, electronics, cables, power equipment, tools, tables, coolers, and support supplies. Assign labels or identifiers where practical. Record expected quantities, assigned trailer, loading position, and condition in Appendix C.

### 5-2. Load plan
Use a written plan for the semi trailer and both smaller trailers. Consider vehicle and trailer limits, weight distribution, securement, fragile items, weather protection, access, and unloading order. Keep exits and safe access clear. Drivers resolve transport concerns before movement; volunteers do not estimate away a capacity or securement concern.

### 5-3. Loading
1. Establish a controlled loading area and clear travel paths.
2. Inspect items before loading; identify damage or missing parts.
3. Load in the approved sequence using sufficient personnel and suitable handling equipment.
4. Secure items to prevent movement and protect delicate components.
5. Reconcile loaded quantities with the event inventory.
6. Complete driver transport checks and designated assistant director load verification.

### 5-4. Unloading and functional verification
The designated equipment assistant ensures proper unloading at the venue and on return. Leads count and inspect items, confirm assembly, and perform appropriate functional checks before use. Missing, unsafe, or nonfunctional equipment is reported immediately and withheld from use until resolved or a director-approved alternative is established.

### 5-5. Release control
Record verification for outbound loading, venue unloading, venue reloading, and home unloading. A crew lead’s initials record task completion; the designated equipment assistant’s signoff records equipment verification. Driver signoff independently confirms transport readiness. Neither signoff replaces the other.

## Chapter 6. Vehicles, Trailers, and Movement

### 6-1. Assignment
Identify the semi tractor/trailer combination and each pickup/trailer combination, driver, backup, destination, and load in Appendix D. Use drivers authorized for the assigned equipment under applicable school requirements.

### 6-2. Departure checks
Drivers complete required vehicle inspections and check coupling, trailer connections, tires, lights, doors, ramps, and load securement as applicable to their equipment. Report defects and resolve readiness concerns before departure. Follow the vehicle’s established inspection and operating procedures.

### 6-3. Movement control
Confirm route, arrival instructions, parking location, contact method, and response to separation or delay. Each driver operates independently and safely; schedule pressure does not justify unsafe travel. Secure loose supplies and tools before movement.

### 6-4. Parking and backing
Keep students and bystanders clear. Use a designated spotter where needed and establish signals before backing. The driver stops if visual contact with the spotter is lost or the path is uncertain. Stabilize and secure parked trailers according to their operating procedures before opening or unloading.

## Chapter 7. Arrival, Unloading, and Site Establishment

### 7-1. Arrival coordination
The Road Crew Chief or designee checks in with event staff and confirms authorized parking, unloading area, equipment routes, serving location, and schedule changes. Notify directors of restrictions affecting equipment or timing.

### 7-2. Unloading control
Complete Appendix E. Establish a clear ramp zone, assign handlers, and unload in sequence. Keep pedestrians away from rolling equipment. Do not open or handle a suspected shifted load until the driver and load lead determine a safe approach.

### 7-3. Site layout
Create separate areas for equipment staging, repairs, meals, and waste. Position meal tables on a stable surface clear of vehicles and equipment routes. Store cases, tools, cables, and coolers so they do not obstruct walkways or emergency access. Secure unattended equipment and protect it from weather.

### 7-4. Ready report
Leads report inventory, condition, and functional status. The designated equipment assistant verifies unloading and equipment readiness and informs the Head Band Director of material deficiencies.

## Chapter 8. Performance Support and Recovery

### 8-1. Preparation
Confirm warm-up, staging, field entry, setup, teardown, and exit timing with directors and event staff. Assign each prop, cart, electronics station, and other crew-supported item to a specific handler or team. Complete Appendix F for each performance.

### 8-2. Execution
Move equipment along approved routes and on the authorized cue. Assemble and position equipment as rehearsed. Check prop stability, wheels and brakes where fitted, cables, and electronics before student use. Refer equipment safety or functionality concerns to the designated equipment assistant.

### 8-3. Recovery
On the authorized cue, clear equipment using assigned teams and routes. Account for loose parts, cables, tools, and accessories. Return items to staging or the loading area, inspect for damage, and report readiness for another performance or reload.

### 8-4. Limitations
Do not improvise structural modifications, electrical work, or new equipment movement methods under time pressure. The designated equipment assistant determines whether an item is ready for use and elevates program-impacting alternatives to the Head Band Director.

## Chapter 9. Meals, Water, and Sustainment

### 9-1. Meal plan
Plan each meal for actual confirmed students, staff, chaperones, drivers, and crew included in the order. Local boxed-meal vendors may include Jimmy John’s, Subway, Chick-fil-A, or another approved vendor. Confirm vendor availability, menu, portions, price, ordering deadline, delivery or pickup, packaging, utensils, condiments, and distribution time.

### 9-2. Event-specific dietary assessment
**Assess dietary accommodations for every event and every planned meal. Do not automatically order a separate meal for every person on a general dietary-restriction list.** A restriction may affect one menu and not another.

For each meal, the Meal Support Lead will:
1. Obtain current needs through the school-approved process and confirm attendance.
2. Compare each participant’s needs with the actual menu, ingredients, preparation, and relevant cross-contact information available from the vendor.
3. Identify whether the standard meal is suitable, needs modification, requires an alternative, or remains unconfirmed.
4. Confirm suitability with the responsible school staff and participant/guardian as appropriate; do not infer safety from a menu name or missing information.
5. Record the approved meal assignment and vendor confirmation in a restricted event worksheet.
6. Reassess substitutions or menu changes before distributing affected meals.

### 9-3. Ordering and receipt
Complete Appendix G before placing the order. Reconcile standard, modified, and alternative meals to the total count. Identify any authorized reserve meals separately. On receipt, check quantities, labels, substitutions, packaging, and vendor handling instructions. Escalate missing or unsuitable meals promptly.

### 9-4. Serving
Provide clean tables so meals are not placed on the ground. Establish an orderly line and assigned servers. Keep identified accommodation meals separate and distribute them to the intended recipients using discreet identifiers. Do not exchange or substitute those meals without confirming suitability. Maintain food under the vendor’s handling guidance and school food-service procedures; arrange collection close to serving time.

### 9-5. Water
Provide drinking water in clean coolers with adequate ice or other approved cooling support, serving supplies, and a replenishment plan. Make water accessible throughout the event, including setup and recovery. Set quantities based on attendance, duration, conditions, refill access, and director guidance. Record the plan in Appendix H.

### 9-6. Cleanup and privacy
Collect waste, clean tables and coolers, and inspect the serving area before departure. Keep personally identifiable dietary and medical details out of general crew copies of this SOP. Use controlled event records for those details.

## Chapter 10. Tools, Repairs, and Maintenance

### 10-1. General repair toolbox
Maintain a portable, labeled toolbox for minor repairs to props, equipment, and trailer accessories within crew qualifications and school authorization. Use Appendix I to inventory tools and consumables. Secure tools during transport and control access at the venue.

### 10-2. Repair process
1. Identify the problem and remove unsafe equipment from use.
2. Notify the Repair Lead and designated equipment assistant.
3. Determine whether an authorized minor repair is appropriate.
4. Perform the repair in a controlled area using suitable tools.
5. Inspect and function-check the item; obtain equipment release from the designated assistant.
6. Record the defect, action, limitation, and required follow-up.

### 10-3. Repair limits
Refer brakes, tires, coupling systems, structural damage, energized electrical work, and other safety-critical repairs to qualified personnel. Tape, ties, or temporary fasteners are not substitutes for structural or transport safety repairs. A temporary repair must have an explicit use limitation and follow-up owner.

### 10-4. Replenishment
After each event, clean tools, recharge batteries, replace consumed supplies, and report missing items. Maintain a list of unresolved repairs before the next event.

## Chapter 11. Safety, Emergencies, and Contingencies

### 11-1. Hazard controls
Brief lifting, pinch points, rolling loads, ramps, unstable props, cables, moving vehicles, weather, heat/cold exposure, and fatigue. Use team lifting or suitable equipment when needed. Keep hands and feet clear of wheels and closing mechanisms. Maintain adequate lighting for night loading.

### 11-2. Emergency response
For immediate danger, stop work, contact emergency services as needed, and notify school staff. Use the event address and access information in Appendix J. Keep access clear and send an assigned adult to guide responders. Follow school procedures for student care, supervision, family notification, and incident reporting. Medical response remains with authorized personnel.

### 11-3. Weather and shelter
Identify the school/event weather decision authority, shelter location, notification method, and equipment protection plan before arrival. On a weather hold, stop affected operations and follow director and venue instructions. Do not delay sheltering to recover equipment.

### 11-4. Contingencies
Plan named contacts and actions for vehicle breakdown, late food, water shortage, missing equipment, equipment failure, unavailable volunteers, schedule changes, and lost communications. Inform directors early when the performance or student support may be affected.

### 11-5. Restart
The responsible lead confirms that the hazard is controlled. The designated equipment assistant verifies affected equipment safety and functionality. The Head Band Director authorizes overall program changes or resumption as required by school/event procedures.

## Chapter 12. Return, Closeout, and Continuous Improvement

### 12-1. Venue departure
Complete Appendix K. Inventory equipment, inspect condition, reload according to plan, secure loads, account for crew, remove waste, and check the site for forgotten items. Obtain designated assistant director verification and driver readiness signoffs before movement.

### 12-2. Home unloading
Establish the unloading area and adequate lighting. Unload and reconcile all equipment, place it in assigned storage, identify damage, and secure vehicles and facilities. The designated equipment assistant verifies proper unloading, equipment condition, and unresolved issues before crew release through the Road Crew Chief.

### 12-3. Administrative closeout
Submit receipts, vendor issues, incident reports, lost-and-found items, inventory changes, and repair requirements to the appropriate school contact. Protect restricted student information and retain records under school procedures.

### 12-4. After-action review
Use Appendix L to record what was planned, what occurred, what worked, what requires improvement, and who owns each action. Review significant findings with directors before the next event.

### 12-5. SOP changes
Revise this working copy chapter by chapter. Record version, date, sections changed, reason, and approval. The Head Band Director approves the SOP; the designated equipment assistant reviews equipment, loading, safety, and functionality provisions. Keep superseded copies clearly marked.
